01 · Decision and scope

CareKlin — provisional brand. Research-stage information architecture and static preview specification. Prepared 18 October 2026. This is a planning document, not an operating website, product, legal clearance, or proof of demand.

Recommended story: One missed visit. Every constraint visible. A dispatcher owns the decision. The exact brief headline remains the primary hero: “Resolve missed home-care visits with a dispatcher-approved exception desk.”

The current request governs: research, complete IA, scroll-story direction and references. The master guide supplies quality standards; it does not authorize a build. Later employee-handoff instructions about browser captures are recorded as future acceptance requirements. Earlier references to an attached screenshot and healthcare.md are unverified: neither was supplied here. No website, account, external system, analytics, or Notion page is changed.

Use CareKlin consistently, superseding Care Kiln/Care Kiln product prefixes. The old carekiln.com RDAP note does not establish availability of a CareKlin domain. Name, domain, trademark, product naming and legal review remain owner decisions. Pennsylvania is a proposed research lens only; choose the actual state with the sponsor before configuring any workflow.

07 · Motion direction and interaction rules

Make the experience motion-rich through choreography, not simultaneous movement. Every major element gets an intentional entrance, interaction or state transition; reading surfaces settle. Rich background motion follows the care thread and day-plan grid instead of particles, glowing orbs or looping medical graphics.

LayerTrigger / behaviorReverse, mobile and reduced-motion
Ambient backgroundSlow line/grid movement while hero is visible; stop while hidden/offscreen; visible motion toggle.Mobile simplifies to one line; reduced-motion shows a still timeline.
Chapter transitionsColor/material shift from warm paper → precise workbench → calm paper; headings reveal as complete lines.Entrance reveals persist after first appearance. Copy is never inaccessible before animation initializes.
Pinned scenarioOne desktop sequence across intake, constraints, approval and draft; scroll progress maps to narrative frames.Up-scroll retraces scripted states. Short/narrow viewports use stacked steps; no forced horizontal navigation.
Cards and controlsBorder rail, 4–8px arrow travel, small evidence highlight, short surface change.Keyboard focus produces equivalent feedback; touch gets explicit controls; no tilt or moving click targets.
Preview actionsExplicit simulation opens review state, gives feedback, allows undo/reset; timeline records fictional owner.Scrolling cannot send or approve. No backend effects.
Data visualizationsHour meter changes only when the named synthetic scenario changes.Always show numbers, units and text; reduced-motion switches instantly.

Suggested timings, to test later: controls 160–220ms; section reveals 450–650ms; small 50–80ms stagger only for short groups. Avoid letter-by-letter body text, continuous card bobbing, camera spins and pointer chasing. Use semantic HTML/SVG for readable workbench text, not generated dashboard images. Native scroll, focus visibility, contrast, pause controls and complete still states are acceptance requirements. GSAP/ScrollTrigger can be evaluated at implementation time; a smooth-scroll library is optional, not a prerequisite.

11 · Validation, interviews, pilot gate and stop rules

Validation sequence: interview buyer, dispatcher and oversight owner separately → document incumbent workflow and observed baseline → replay synthetic example and test price response → seek bounded export/data rights for human-reviewed retrospective replay → agree endpoint, guardrails and explicit stop rule → consider a governed pilot. Shadowing and anonymized replay permissions are future commitments, not completed evidence.

Interview groupPrompts
COO / staffing directorWho owns missed visits and budget? How is coverage currently measured? What does the incumbent already solve? Which branch/state is feasible? Who can approve a paid pilot and data access? How would you assess $650/month plus $3,000 setup against per-visit economics? What would make this unnecessary?
DispatcherWalk through the most recent missed shift without identifiers. Which source is authoritative? How do skill, availability, travel, preferences and authorized hours get checked? Where are timestamps lost? When do you stop searching? Who approves a replacement and communication?
Oversight / rule ownerWhich state, payer and service rules apply? Who owns interpretation and updates? What worker consent/policy and labor constraints apply? Which read/write/export rights exist? How are EVV discrepancies and fraud controls handled? What evidence, retention, security and human oversight are prerequisites?

Readiness gate: 8 dispatch/COO interviews in one state; 2 missed-shift replay permissions; confirmed EVV route; 1 paid branch pilot commitment; signed data-access/security and human-oversight approvals. Also confirm one geography, agency system of record, vendor interface, procurement owner, worker consent/policy, named state-rule owner, written scope, endpoint and review gate. A paid commitment is not evidence of efficacy.

Primary metric: percentage of missed visits with dispatcher-approved feasible coverage before scheduled start versus baseline. Numerator: eligible missed visits with a documented feasible replacement approved before start. Denominator: all missed visits meeting pre-agreed cohort criteria within the evaluation window, including failures. Record discovery time; separately report cases first discovered after scheduled start rather than quietly excluding them. Define equal, comparable baseline/pilot windows and case mix with the sponsor before measurement.

Guardrails: authorization breaches, dispatcher minutes (including review/corrections), and unfilled visit count/rate. Record sample size, missing evidence, source, decision-maker role, exclusions and uncertainty. A synthetic walkthrough reports no improvement percentage, savings or clinical outcome.

Kill or reposition: no EVV vendor route and named state-rule owner; suggestions violate travel, skill or authorized hours. Stop affected recommendations immediately, investigate and require review before resuming. Also pause if permissions are absent or interpretation misleads buyers. Expansion to multi-branch staffing, authorization/EVV operations and payer quality reporting remains a hypothesis dependent on interoperability and workforce economics. No caregiver-count TAM, million-dollar forecast, reimbursement or regulatory conclusion.
